# PREFACE. (continued)

HELAYUDDHA[?] BHATT'A: it is a vocabulary of limited extent, divided into five books; the first relating to Heaven and the Deities, and the second to Earth: the third to Hell, Water, &c.; the fourth to miscellaneous objects, and the last contains words of various signification: the author cites as his authorities, the *Ameradatta* and the *Cóshas* of VARARUCHI, BHA'GURI, VO'PALITA and others, (वोपालि[illegible] ।): his work should be of some antiquity as he is quoted by MALLINA'T'H, who is certainly prior to ME'DINI'CARA[?]: his omission of the *Amera Cósha*, from his list of authorities, can scarcely lead us to infer the priority of his own work, but it is possible, that the reputation of that vocabulary was not fully established in his time, and, that it is therefore included in the appendix *A'di*, or etcetera, added to his enumeration. HALAYUDHA BHATT'A\* has also written on Prosody, and there is a celebrated writer of the same name, on the religious ceremonies of the *Hindus*: in both instances honourable mention has been made by Mr. COLEBROOKE of these works: he is said to be a *Concana Brahman* and the pupil of the *Mímánsá* writer, BHATT'A BHA'SCARA; if this last account is correct, he may be placed about the seventh century, as his preceptor is uniformly said to have preceded S'ANCARA A'CHA'RYA.

AJAYA or AJAYAPA'LA, is the next in the list of the *Médiní*, whose vocabulary has been consulted in the following pages: this is a work of small extent, but of considerable authority: it is confined to the *Nánárt'ha*, or words of various meanings, and they are arranged according to the initial letters: the author does not specify his authorities, nor does he furnish us with any clue to his age or history; from his introductory stanza, he appears to be a sectary, probably a *Jaina*;† the work is often cited by the commentators on the *Amera Cósha*.

The next in the list of the *Médiní*, is the *Dharan'í Cósha* or vocabulary of DHARAN'I DA'S, an author, of whom we know nothing further, except that he is considered as a *Brahman* of *Cánouj*, and the author of a poetical work, denominated the *Cás'í Virudávalí*: the vocabulary is of some extent, although it is limited to words of various meanings, arranged according to their final letters and number of syllables: the author specifies no authorities, and states only generally that his vocabulary is compiled from older writers: he is himself of some antiquity, as besides the place he holds in *Médiní's* list, he is often cited by MALLI NA'T'HA.

---

\* *Asiatic Researches* VII. 233 and X. 391.

† In a paper in the *Transactions of the Literary Society of Bombay*, by LIEUTENANT MACKMURDO, which I shall again have occasion to notice, an AJEE PA'L occurs, who seems to have taken an active part in the introduction of the *Jaina* Religion into *Guzerat*, towards the end of the twelfth century, and who may have been the compiler of the vocabulary, or the patron of its author, who consequently ascribes it to him; a practice frequent in the literary flattery of *Hindu* writers.

_Dr. Mārcis Gasūns_
