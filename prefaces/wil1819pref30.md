# PREFACE. (continued)

The *Tricán'da Sés'ha*, is a vocabulary, in three chapters, composed purposely as a supplement to the vocabulary of AMERA, and thence sometimes termed the *Amera Sés'ha*; it follows a similar arrangement, and adds a number of words of comparatively rare occurrence. The author was a *Jaina*, named PURUSHOTTAMA DE'VA, and he is the author also of several other philological compilations as an *E'cácshara Cósha* or vocabulary of monosyllabic words, which I have occasionally referred to, and the *Vern'a Viplava Nás'a*[?], or a collation of various readings: his most celebrated work, however, after the *Tricán'da*, is the following, which indeed takes precedence of it in the list of the *Médiní*.

The *Hárávalí*, is a collection almost exclusively of uncommon words: it is divided into two parts; the one containing synonimous words, and the other words of various meanings: the first part is again divided into three portions, according to the extent of the synonimes, and their recapitulation through a whole stanza, or a half, or a quarter; the work is of very limited extent, but it is the produce, according to the author's own averment, of twelve years reading; he states also, that it was compiled at the suggestion, and with the aid of his spiritual preceptor, SRI' DHRITI SINH, an *Atít'hi* or religious ascetic, known in the spoken dialects by the appellation *Atít*, and he mentions JANAMEJAYA, a *Pánd'it*, as having contributed to the compilation: the authorities he alludes to, are mostly of a general and miscellaneous description, but amongst them he enumerates, as the *Cóshas* consulted, and consequently prior to his time, the *S'abdárn'ava*, *Utpaliní*, SANSA'RAVERTA and those of VA'CHESPATI VYA'DI and VICRAMA'DITYA: the age of PURUSHOTTAMA according to the order of the *Médiní*, should be earlier than that of the *Vis'va*, and he may probably have written in the tenth or eleventh century: he is not however quoted by the commentator MALLINA'T'H, who cites the *Vis'va Cósha*.

After the *Tricán'da Sés'ha*, we have in the *Médiní's* list, a work entitled the *Retna Mála*, I have employed a *Cósha* of this name, but I am not quite confident of its being the same; the *Retna Mála* of IRUGAPA, entitled *Dan'd'ádhi Nát'ha*[?], said to imply, as it may signify, a military officer of high rank, and probably the patron rather than the author of the work: it is a compilation of considerable authority, especially in the South of *India*, and its age may be ascertained with tolerable precision, for it is said to have been undertaken at the desire of HARIHARA, a prince, who conjointly with BUCCA RA'YA,\* his brother, founded the city of *Vidyanagar* or *Bijnagar*, about 1340 and reigned there till 1350.
