The commentary which takes precedence of the rest by the earlier date of its composition, is that of CSHI'RA SWA'MI': it is a work of merit but concise; and the interpretations frequently differ from those given by the other commentators: the age of the author is not known, but he is one of the authorities cited by MALLI NA'TH the commentator on several of the most celebrated poems, and who probably wrote in the twelfth or thirteenth century.

The next in the order of antiquity is the work of MUCUTA or RA'YA MUCUT'A MAN'I, compiled according to the author's own account from sixteen older commentaries to which he frequently refers: they seem however to be in many cases original compositions, and not merely glosses on the text of AMERA: the list includes the works of CSHI'RA SWA'MI', SUBHU'TI, HAD'D'A CHANDRA, CALINGA, CONCATA, SARVADHARA, RA'JADE'VA, GOVERDHANA, DRA'VID'A, and BHO'JA RA'JA, and the *Vyác'hyámrĭta*, *T'ícasarvasva*, *Má'dhaví*, *Madhu Má'dhavi*, *Sarvánanda*, and *Abhinanda*. MUCUT'A also cites the names of the principal grammatical writers, and quotes many *Cóshas* of which the titles are of frequent and familiar occurrence, amongst which, some as the *Vis'va* and *Médiní* are still in existence: the commentary of RA'YA MUCUT'A, is full and satisfactory—it is erroneous sometimes in the etymological analysis of grammatical derivatives, but it is in general accurate, and in most of the schools is of high authority: the date of the composition is inferred from dates mentioned by the author, 1352 *Saca* or A. D. 1430.*

The *Vyác'hyá S'udhi* is the ablest commentary on the *Amera Cósha*, that has yet appeared: the interpretations are full and distinct, and the etymological analysis founded on the most thorough acquaintance with the system of PA'N'INI: the commentator is also a very industrious corrector of the mistakes of his brethren, and rectifies especially the grammatical errors of his predecessor MUCUTA: in compliance with the usual practice I have referred to this writer under the appellation RA'MA'S'RAMA, but as is mentioned by Mr. COLEBROOKE, the name of the author is variously written in different copies and is sometimes the same with that which I have used, and is sometimes termed BHA'NU DI'CSHITA: in the copy I have used I find an apparent solution of this difficul-culty, and that *Rámás'rama* or more correctly *Bhadra Rámás'rama* is a title, implying the author's belonging to a particular class of devotees, whilst his real name is BHA'NU DI'CSHITA and he is the son, as he informs us, of BHAT'T'OJI DI'CSHITA, the celebrated grammarian: I must add
